How are local firms affected by the entry of products that are of low quality and relatively cheaper? This paper provides a theoretical and empirical investigation of the differential impact of the import penetration of low quality products on the market power of local firms with different levels of quality. In the model, the market hit by international competition is segmented into low- and high-quality firms, with differential demand elasticities across firm sizes through CREMR preferences. The model predicts that import penetration of low quality goods has a stronger, negative impact on the markups of local low-quality producers. This prediction is supported by a difference-in-differences estimation on US Compustat data around China's WTO accession. Local low-quality firms respond by investing more in innovation to upgrade quality, while high-quality firms invest more in advertising to signal their relative superiority.
