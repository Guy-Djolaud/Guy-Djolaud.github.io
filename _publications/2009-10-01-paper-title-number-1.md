This paper provides the first time-series analysis of labour market concentration in Canada. Using linked employer–employee data for 2001–2019 and a wage-bill Herfindahl index, I show that national and local concentration declined over the long run but diverged during the Great Recession, when aggregate local concentration rose by 14 per cent. Economy-wide, Public Administration accounts for 54 per cent of the long-run within-province decline in local concentration, consistent with the federal restructuring that followed Budget 2012, and wage-bill reallocation toward this highly concentrated sector explains 68 per cent of the within-province rise during the recession. In the private sector, the long-run decline comes from falling concentration within industries, reinforced by a shift of the wage bill toward less concentrated industries; net firm entry offsets about a quarter of the decline generated by continuing firms. During the recession, private-sector concentration rose mainly within industries, and entry and exit amplified the increase. With a leave-one-out instrument, the market-level firm wage premium falls with concentration, with an elasticity of −0.028. Finally, a two-agent New Keynesian model with an endogenous wage markdown shows that government spending narrows markdowns and shifts income from profits to wages, more strongly than in a model without cyclical monopsony.
